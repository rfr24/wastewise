The problem

Evans Hall waste stations are labelled inconsistently. A student has a coffee cup, a wrapper, and thirty seconds.

From informal conversations with SOM classmates, three recurring confusions:

  • Coffee cups — assumed recyclable because they are paper. Yale excludes them.
  • "Compostable" packaging — assumed to belong in compost, regardless of whether the building collects any.
  • Contamination anxiety — not knowing whether a wrong guess makes things worse, so people default to trash.
Qualitative motivation from peer conversations — not a survey, and not a measured result.

Safety

The real risk is confidently wrong advice, which converts uncertainty into misplaced trust.

  • Code outranks the model. enforceVerification() re-checks confidence against the rules actually cited and downgrades overstatement.
  • Hazards are over-blocked on purpose. Batteries and chemicals never get a station bin, even when the model is confident.
  • Injection: user text is data, not instruction — including text visible inside a photo. Uploads verified by magic bytes.
  • Cost: per-IP rate limit, hard daily cap on billable calls.

A battery is kept out of every station bin and routed to Yale EHS.

A defence that caught a real failure: a bare "paper" alias made the fast path answer "paper plate → recycling, confirmed." Fixed structurally — generic words can no longer carry a match — and locked down by regression tests.

Impact — the Facilities loop

The durable value isn't one answer. It's that the questions students ask are data a Sustainability Chair has never had.

  • Recurring questions → where signage fails
  • Unresolved answers → where guidance doesn't exist
  • Thumbs-down → wording problems, not rules problems

Four opportunities, each with its own evidence and caveats.

No student testing has happened. Every recorded question but one is my own development testing, and Facilities has not adopted this tool. The loop above is a plan for my role, not an arrangement that exists.
