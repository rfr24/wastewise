The Problem

Standing at the bin with thirty seconds

As Yale SOM's 1Y Sustainability Chair, I kept noticing that waste stations across Evans Hall are labelled inconsistently — and that classmates were unsure what actually goes where.

Informal conversations with classmates revealed three recurring sources of confusion:

  • Takeaway coffee cups — assumed recyclable because they are paper. Yale lists paper hot cups as not accepted.
  • "Compostable" packaging — the label is assumed to mean the bin. It doesn't: Yale's published guidance sends compostable utensils, plates and cups to the trash, and acceptance otherwise depends on the material and the collection programme. What Evans Hall actually collects is unconfirmed.
  • "Recycling it is the safer guess" — the belief that an uncertain item is better off in recycling. The wrong item can contaminate a whole load.

Based on informal conversations with Yale SOM classmates — not a survey or a study.

How WasteWise Works

Built for reliability. Straightforward questions can be answered directly from verified rules without an AI call. More complex questions use AI alongside retrieved guidance, with code-based safety checks before the response is shown.

Safety and Reliability

Grounded guidance and safeguards, never a confident guess

  • Code outranks the model. Stated confidence is re-checked against the rules actually cited, and downgraded if overstated.
  • Hazards are over-blocked on purpose. Batteries and chemicals never get a station bin, even when the model is confident.
  • Injection: user text is data, not instruction — including text inside a photo.
  • Cost: per-IP rate limit and a hard daily cap.
What testing caught. An early version answered "paper plate → recycling, confirmed": the plate matched a rule about clean office paper on the bare word "paper", and that match looked certain enough to skip the AI step entirely. Testing exposed it, and the fix was structural — generic material words can no longer carry a match on their own, and catch-all rules are barred from the no-AI path. Paper plates now correctly route to trash, and regression tests hold it there. 335 tests pass.

Testing and Potential Impact

Demonstrated. The app works end to end: 24 curated rules, text and photo sorting, guardrails that hold, and a dashboard that turns questions into concrete opportunities. Friends have tried it informally and given feedback that shaped the wording and the photo flow.

Potential, not yet measured. Yale SOM Facilities is the building operations team responsible for managing or coordinating waste collection and bin infrastructure — their exact remit is still to be confirmed. Patterns in what students ask could help them spot unclear signage, knowledge gaps, and chances to improve guidance.

It has not been distributed to SOM students or formally evaluated. No Facilities-driven change and no contamination reduction has been measured. The dashboard figures are predominantly my own development testing, which the dashboard states on every view.
