Standing at the bin with thirty seconds

As Yale SOM's 1Y Sustainability Chair, I kept noticing the same two things: waste stations across Evans Hall are labelled inconsistently, and classmates were unsure what actually goes where.

Three confusions came up again and again:

  • Takeaway coffee cups — assumed recyclable because they are paper. Yale lists paper hot cups as not accepted.
  • "Compostable" packaging — assumed to belong in compost. Whether that is right depends on what Evans Hall actually collects, which is not confirmed.
  • "Recycling it is the safer guess" — the belief that an uncertain item is better off in recycling. The wrong item can contaminate a whole load.

Based on informal conversations with Yale SOM classmates.

How it works

An easy, verified text question never reaches a model at all. Everything else is retrieved first, reasoned over second, and re-checked in code last — the bin always comes from the knowledge base, never from the model's own knowledge.

Safety

The real risk is confidently wrong advice, which turns uncertainty into misplaced trust.

  • Code outranks the model. Confidence is re-checked against the rules actually cited, and downgraded if overstated.
  • Hazards are over-blocked on purpose. Batteries and chemicals never get a station bin, even when the model is confident.
  • Injection: user text is data, not instruction — including text inside a photo.
  • Cost: per-IP rate limit and a hard daily cap.

A battery is kept out of every station bin and routed to Yale EHS.

A defence that caught a real failure: a bare "paper" alias once let the fast path answer "paper plate → recycling, confirmed." Fixed structurally and locked down by regression tests. 335 tests pass.

Impact and testing

Demonstrated. The app works end to end: 24 cited rules, text and photo sorting, guardrails that hold, and a dashboard that turns questions into four concrete opportunities. Friends have tried it informally and given feedback that shaped the wording and the photo flow.

Potential, not yet measured. Recurring questions point at failing signage, unresolved answers at missing guidance, and thumbs-down at wording problems — a standing input to Facilities conversations.

Two of the four opportunities surfaced, evidence expanded. The first is what led to the verified rule behind the demo on slide 5.

It has not been distributed to SOM students or formally evaluated, and nothing here measures sorting behaviour, contamination or diversion. The dashboard figures are predominantly my own development testing, which the dashboard states on every view.
